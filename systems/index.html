Why is this high? · Conductivity

▲ Too high

Likely causes

  • Bleed valve stuck closed or plugged.
  • Bleed controller off or in hand/off.
  • Makeup water saltier than usual.
  • Too little bleed after a hot spell.

Look and check, in this order

  1. Is the reading itself wrong? Use the shared steps below. Retest and calibration steps
  2. Look at the bleed valve. Is water draining? (High: it should be. Low: is it running nonstop?)
  3. Look at the controller screen. Does the bleed setpoint match the posted sheet? Look only, don't touch.
  4. Walk the basin. Is it overflowing, leaking, or is the makeup valve running nonstop?
  5. Write what you saw.

Call the rep: Still out after the retest; any leak or overflow; the setpoint looks different from the sheet; or cycles by conductivity and by chloride disagree a lot.

Why is this low? · Free halogen

▼ Too low

Likely causes

  • Drum empty or lost strength (old bleach goes weak).
  • Pump lost prime.
  • Timer or ORP control off.
  • Very dirty water using it up (high dip slide).
  • Hot sun on an open basin.
  • A lot of new makeup water.

Look and check, in this order

  1. Is the reading itself wrong? Test within minutes with fresh DPD. High sulfite or manganese can fool it (typical). Retest and calibration steps
  2. Look at the drum level and whether the pump is clicking.
  3. Look at the biocide timer or ORP setpoint against the posted sheet. Look only.
  4. Test again in 30 minutes.

Call the rep: Still low after 30 minutes: call today. If it's high, keep people out of the drift and call today.

Why is this low? · Inhibitor

▼ Too low

Likely causes

  • A leak, so fresh makeup water is thinning the loop.
  • A recent drain and refill (repair work) with no re-treat.
  • Glycol added or swapped.
  • Nitrite eaten by bacteria (often with a bad smell or slime).
  • Pot feeder left open and flushed.

Look and check, in this order

  1. Is the reading itself wrong? Right kit for nitrite vs molybdate; fresh reagent. If glycol is in the loop, ask the rep if the kit works with it. Retest and calibration steps
  2. Read the loop makeup water meter and compare with the last log. If it moved, water is leaking out somewhere. Look for drips at pumps, valves, coils.
  3. Look at the pot feeder. Are the valves closed the way the posted sheet shows? Look only.
  4. Ask maintenance: was the loop drained, refilled, or glycol added since the last test?
  5. Look at the sample color. Brown or black water, or rust flakes, means rust. Write it down.

Call the rep: Still out after the retest; the makeup meter is moving; any drain/refill or glycol change. Brown/black water or a rotten-egg smell: call today.

You look, test, and call. Only the rep changes a pump, a dose, or a setpoint.

Is the reading itself wrong?

Do this first, every time. It takes about 5 minutes.

Show the 7 checks
  • Take a fresh sample. Use a clean bottle rinsed 3 times with the same water. Let the line run about 30 seconds first. Test within 15 minutes (typical). free halogen and sulfite go bad fast. Fill to the top with no air bubbles.
  • Check the reagents. Look at the expiration date. Did it change color? Was it stored hot or frozen? If it's old, use a new pack.
  • Check the meter. Is the probe clean and wet? Was it calibrated when the rep's sheet says (pH buffers 4/7/10 are typical; use the conductivity standard your rep gave you)? Is the battery OK?
  • Check the temperature. A hot boiler sample must go through the sample cooler first, to room temperature. Hot water reads wrong and can burn you.
  • Controller vs handheld: if the two disagree by more than the rep's limit (about 10% is typical until the rep sets it), trust neither. Write down both numbers and call the rep. Don't recalibrate the controller unless the rep trained you to.
  • Known mix-ups (typical): high sulfite or manganese can make a free halogen test read high. Strong color or cloudiness can throw off color tests. If the sample looks odd, write it down.
  • Same answer twice? It's real. Go to the checks.

If the retest matches, or the number has moved the same way for 2 rounds, treat it as a real problem, not a bad reading.

Inspection-ready logs

SPS 341: low-pressure steam and hot-water boilers are inspected every 36 months, power boilers every 12. Safety valves are tested every 12 months. On CSD-1 equipment the inspector looks at the boiler log and the operator's checklist, so keep them filled in. Post the permit to operate near the boiler.
